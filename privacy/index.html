Privacy policy of Peekel for Android

Peekel is a book reader for Android. This policy says what it does with your data. In short: your books and settings stay on your device. The app sends anonymous statistics of its use and reports of its crashes, which you can turn off at any time, and which never hold your books, the words you read or the addresses of your servers.

What the app keeps, on your device only

  • The books you add (copied into the app's own storage), their covers, where you are in each book, your bookmarks and settings.
  • The network sources you add (a shared folder, WebDAV, a catalogue): their addresses and user names, and passwords, which are sealed with the Android keystore of your device.

None of this is sent to us. If Android's own backup of apps is on for your device, Android may keep the library (where you are in each book), the settings and the sources in your Google account, as it does for other apps; the books themselves are left out, and a password sealed by the keystore cannot be opened on another device. Removing the app removes all of it from the device.

What goes over the network, and only when you ask

  • The sources you open: the app connects to the catalogue, shared folder or WebDAV server you chose (or one of the catalogues it comes with: Project Gutenberg, Wikisource, ManyBooks, and ImWerden if you add it) to list and download books. Those servers see what any visit sees (your address, what is asked for) and follow their own policies.
  • Finding shared folders: "Find in the network" asks the devices of your home network, and only those, which of them share folders.
  • Uploading from a browser: while that screen is open, the app listens in your home network for a browser that knows the code shown on the screen, and takes the books it sends.

Statistics and crash reports, and how to turn them off

They are on from the start. To turn them off, open Settings, "Privacy", and turn off "Send anonymous statistics": the app sends one last note that they were turned off, with nothing else in it, and from then on nothing is sent.

While they are on, the app uses Google's Firebase Analytics and Firebase Crashlytics to send:

  • which formats are opened, how long the first page took and how long the book is (in rough steps, not exact numbers), and whether a book could not be opened (its format only);
  • how books are added (from files, a folder, another app, a browser, the network) and how many (in rough steps);
  • which kind of network source is added (a catalogue, WebDAV, a shared folder), and whether it was found or typed;
  • which settings are turned on or off;
  • which parts of the reader are used (the contents, bookmarks, search, zoom, a word looked up), never what was searched or looked up;
  • how long a book is read at a time and how many pages are turned (in rough steps), the look of the page (day, sepia, night), and whether a book was read to its end;
  • about how many books the library holds (in rough steps);
  • reports of crashes and of errors of the reading code, with the app's state at the time (never the book);
  • what Firebase gathers for every app: when the app was first opened, how long it is used, its version, the device's model, Android version, language and country, and an identifier of this installation of the app (not of you, not the advertising id).

Never sent: the titles, authors or files of your books, their text, the words you look up, the addresses, user names or passwords of your sources. The app does not use the advertising id, and nothing is used for adverts. Google processes this data for us under its own terms (firebase.google.com/support/privacy).

Other apps

A word you look up, or a request you send, goes to the app you choose on your device (a translator, a dictionary, an assistant), through Android's own sharing. What that app does with it is under its own policy.

What the app does not do

No account, no adverts, no advertising id, no data sold. No statistics once you turn them off. The app's permissions are for the internet (the sources above, and the statistics).

Children

The app is not aimed at children.
